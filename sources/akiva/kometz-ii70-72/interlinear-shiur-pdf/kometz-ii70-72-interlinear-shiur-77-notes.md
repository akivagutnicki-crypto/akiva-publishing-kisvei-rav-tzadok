**Part Two — Sections 70 through 72 (Parshas Vayeitzei)**

**Dedication & Introduction:** This ⁧שִׁעוּר⁩ is dedicated ⁧לְעִלּוּי נִשְׁמַת דְּבוֹרָה חַנָּה בַּת הָרַב נֹחַ⁩ (mother of Justin Eichel, who passed away three years ago)—may her ⁧נְשָׁמָה⁩ have an ⁧עֲלִיָּה⁩. Drawn from the ⁧כְּתַב יָד⁩ of ⁧רַב צָדוֹק הַכֹּהֵן מִלּוּבְּלִין⁩ in ⁧קֹמֶץ הַמִּנְחָה⁩ on ⁧פָּרָשַׁת וַיֵּצֵא⁩, these three sections offer a poetic, overarching lens on providence, inner wholeness, and spiritual growth through opposition and descent.

**Section 70 (אות ע׳) — וַיֵּצֵא יַעֲקֹב מִבְּאֵר שֶׁבַע וַיֵּלֶךְ חָרָנָה**

**1\. Two Objectives Unified on One Day (⁧בֶּן יוֹמוֹ⁩)**

״וַיֵּצֵא יַעֲקֹב מִבְּאֵר שָׁבַע וַיֵּלֶךְ חָרָנָה״,[^1] **“Jacob departed from Beer-sheba and went to Haran.”** — כִּי הָיוּ לוֹ שְׁנֵי מְכֻוָּנִים: **For he had two objectives:** — הַיְצִיאָה, שֶׁהוּא צִוּוּי רִבְקָה, **his departure, which was Rebecca’s command,** — רַק הַבְּרִיחָה מִשָּׁם, **was simply to flee from there,** — וְלֹא לְתַכְלִית הַזִּוּוּג שָׁם; **and was not intended to bring about his marriage there;** — וּכְמוֹ שֶׁאָמְרָה, שֶׁתִּשְׁלַח אַחֲרָיו אַחַר כָּךְ, **as she said, she would send for him afterward,** — רַק לֹא הִגִּידָה זֶה לְיִצְחָק. **but she did not tell Isaac this.** — וּמְכֻוָּן צִוּוּי לְיִצְחָק **The purpose of Isaac’s command** — רַק הַהֲלִיכָה לְחָרָן לְזִוּוּג.[^2] **was solely that he go to Haran to marry.** — וּשְׁנֵיהֶם הָיוּ מְכֻוָּן אֶחָד מַמָּשׁ, **Yet the two were truly a single purpose.** — וְזֶה שֶׁאָמְרוּ זִכְרוֹנָם לִבְרָכָה: **This is the meaning of our Sages’ statement:** — ״בֶּן יוֹמוֹ״,[^3] **“On that very day.”** — רָצָה לוֹמַר, שֶׁהַיּוֹם גּוֹרֵם וּמְיֻחָד לְכָל מַעֲשֶׂה, **That is, the day contributes to each deed and is specially appointed for it.** — וְכָאן ״בֶּן יוֹמוֹ״, שֶׁהַכֹּל מְכֻוָּן אֶחָד. **Here, “on that very day” means that everything serves one purpose.**

**Shiur Commentary:** The ⁧מְפָרְשִׁים⁩ all ask why the ⁧פָּסוּק⁩ uses two separate verbs—⁧וַיֵּצֵא⁩ (departed) and ⁧וַיֵּלֶךְ⁩ (went). ⁧רַב צָדוֹק⁩ explains that they reflect two distinct commands: ⁧רִבְקָה⁩’s ⁧צִוּוּי⁩ was purely a ⁧בְּרִיחָה⁩ (escape from ⁧עֵשָׂו⁩), while ⁧יִצְחָק⁩’s ⁧צִוּוּי⁩ was specifically to travel to ⁧חָרָן⁩ for a ⁧זִוּוּג⁩. Yet in ⁧הַשְׁגָּחָה⁩, both motives were one unified ⁧תַּכְלִית⁩.

Applying the phrase ⁧בֶּן יוֹמוֹ⁩ (familiar also from ⁧כַּשְׁרוּת⁩) to life, ⁧רַב צָדוֹק⁩ teaches a meta-lesson: certain pivotal days in our personal history act as a trigger—a chain reaction of ⁧הַשְׁגָּחָה⁩. Events that appear unrelated on the surface, such as a chance meeting on the same day as another turning point, are appointed by that day itself to work together as a larger picture that makes us ⁧שָׁלֵם⁩.

**2\. Aram, Bnei Kedem, and Help That Stands Opposite (⁧עֵזֶר כְּנֶגְדּוֹ⁩)**

כִּי הַזִּוּוּגוֹ הוּא רַק בַּאֲרָם, מְקוֹם הָרַמָּאוּת, **For his marriage could take place only in Aram, a place of deceit,** — וּבִבְנֵי קֶדֶם, מְקוֹם הַנִּחוּשִׁים, **and among the people of the East, a place of divination,** — הֵפֶךְ יַעֲקֹב, ״אִישׁ תָּם״,[^4] **the opposite of Jacob, “a man of integrity,”** — וְ״לֹא נַחַשׁ בְּיַעֲקֹב״.[^5] **and of the declaration, “There is no divination in Jacob.”** — וְכַנִּזְכָּר לְעֵיל אוֹת נ׳, **As mentioned above in section 50,** — כִּי הָעֵזֶר רַק כַּאֲשֶׁר הוּא כְּנֶגְדּוֹ, **help comes precisely when it stands opposite him.** — וְהַיְנוּ, הָעֵזֶר לִשְׁלֵמוּת הוּא מִצַּד הַנֶּגְדִּי לוֹ; **That is, help toward perfection comes from what is contrary to him.** — כַּאֲשֶׁר הוּא גַּם כֵּן בִּשְׁלֵמוּת, **When he is complete in that respect as well,** — אָז גַּם כֵּן מְבֹרָר בִּשְׁלֵמוּת,[^6] **his perfection is then also established.** — כְּדֶרֶךְ שֶׁכָּתַבְתִּי לְעֵיל כַּמָּה פְּעָמִים **This follows what I wrote above several times** — עַל נִסְיוֹנוֹת אַבְרָהָם, שֶׁהָיוּ בְּהֵפֶךְ מִטִּבְעוֹ וּמִדָּתוֹ.[^7] **about Abraham’s trials, which ran contrary to his nature and characteristic quality.**

**Shiur Commentary:** The very name ⁧אֲרָם⁩ shares its letters with ⁧רַמַּאי⁩ (deceit), and ⁧בְּנֵי קֶדֶם⁩ represents a culture of ⁧נִחוּשִׁים⁩ (divination)—the complete antithesis of ⁧יַעֲקֹב אִישׁ תָּם⁩. (In class discussion, a participant noted geographically that ⁧חָרָן⁩ lies north of ⁧אֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל⁩ at the northern curve of the Fertile Crescent near Turkey rather than due east, while traditional ⁧מְפָרְשִׁים⁩ and the ⁧גְּמָרָא⁩ render ⁧קֶדֶם⁩ as the East, including ⁧בָּבֶל⁩.)

⁧רַב צָדוֹק⁩ here defines ⁧עֵזֶר כְּנֶגְדּוֹ⁩ in its deepest sense: true help toward ⁧שְׁלֵמוּת⁩ comes specifically from what opposes your natural ⁧מִדָּה⁩. Just as ⁧אַבְרָהָם⁩’s ⁧נִסְיוֹנוֹת⁩ ran counter to his natural ⁧חֶסֶד⁩, ⁧יַעֲקֹב⁩’s entire life—from the lentils and the ⁧בְּרָכוֹת⁩, through ⁧לָבָן⁩ and his marriages, to ⁧עֵשָׂו⁩, the brothers’ concealment of ⁧יוֹסֵף⁩, and even the shift of the ⁧בְּכוֹרָה⁩ (⁧רְאוּבֵן⁩ to ⁧יְהוּדָה⁩, ⁧מְנַשֶּׁה⁩ to ⁧אֶפְרַיִם⁩)—was shaped by encountering his opposite, sharpening his ⁧חֶסְרוֹנוֹת⁩ and elevating his own ⁧מַעֲלָה⁩.

**3\. Clarification Through Cunning (⁧רַמָּאוּת⁩) and Worthiness for Blessing**

וּלְכָךְ הִגִּיעַ יַעֲקֹב לַבְּרָכוֹת גַּם כֵּן עַל יְדֵי רַמָּאוּת. **Therefore Jacob also attained the blessings through cunning.** — (וְכֵן לְזִוּוּגוֹ — רַמָּאוּת, **(Likewise, his marriage involved cunning,** — שֶׁעִקַּר הַבְּרִיחָה, כְּמוֹ שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר: ״וַיִּבְרַח יַעֲקֹב שְׂדֵה אֲרָם״,[^8] **for the journey was fundamentally a flight, as it says, “Jacob fled to the field of Aram,”** — וְרִמְּתָה רִבְקָה לְיִצְחָק; **and Rebecca deceived Isaac.** — וְכֵן הָרְכוּשׁ — רִמָּה בְּלָבָן בַּמַּקְלוֹת; **Likewise, to gain his possessions, he outwitted Laban through the rods.** — וְלֵאָה, עֲקֶרֶת הַבַּיִת, עִקַּר מְסֻבִּין — הִגִּיעָה בְּרַמָּאוּת;[^9] **And Leah, the mainstay of the household, from whom its principal figures came, was obtained through deception.** — שֶׁכָּל שְׁלֵמוּתוֹ רַק עַל יְדֵי רַמָּאוּת, **Thus all his perfection came specifically through cunning,** — שֶׁהוּא הֵפֶךְ מִדָּתוֹ, שֶׁזֶּהוּ הָעֵזֶר כְּנֶגְדּוֹ.) **which is the opposite of his characteristic quality; this is the help that stands opposite him.)** — וְהַיְנוּ שֶׁיֵּשׁ לוֹ רַמָּאוּת בִּמְקוֹם שֶׁצָּרִיךְ,[^10] **That is, he possesses cunning where it is needed,** — וְאָז גַּם מִצַּד תַּמּוּת מְבֹרָר שֶׁרָאוּי לִבְרָכָה.[^11] **and then, even in respect of integrity, he is shown to be worthy of blessing.** — נִמְצָא, בֵּרוּר יַעֲקֹב רַק מִצַּד הָרַמָּאוּת, **It follows that Jacob is refined precisely through cunning,** — שֶׁהוּא הֶעְדֵּר הַשְּׁלֵמוּת וְהַתְּמִימוּת, **which is the absence of perfection and integrity,** — שֶׁגַּם זֶה רַק טוֹב.[^12] **so that this too is entirely good.**

**Shiur Commentary:** When asked in class about ⁧עִקַּר מְסֻבִּין⁩, the ⁧שִׁעוּר⁩ explained that ⁧מְסֻבִּין⁩ refers to prominent, great men (citing the ⁧מִדְרָשׁ⁩ in footnote 9). Asked whether this means ⁧רִבְקָה⁩ herself acted with ⁧רַמָּאוּת⁩, the ⁧שִׁעוּר⁩ noted that ⁧רִבְקָה⁩ grew up in the house of ⁧בְּתוּאֵל⁩ (where they even plotted against ⁧אֱלִיעֶזֶר⁩), and that ⁧אֱמֶת⁩ on a deeper level sometimes requires knowing how to wield ⁧רַמָּאוּת בִּמְקוֹם שֶׁצָּרִיךְ⁩ for the sake of ⁧אֱמֶת⁩.

This theme runs from ⁧תּוֹלְדוֹת⁩ all the way through ⁧וַיִּגַּשׁ⁩: ⁧יוֹסֵף⁩’s elaborate setup with his brothers was “⁧יַעֲקֹב⁩ on steroids,” and the entire story of ⁧פּוּרִים⁩ and ⁧אֶסְתֵּר⁩ likewise unfolded through strategic concealment. (The class also shared a lighthearted moment on marital ⁧עֵזֶר כְּנֶגְדּוֹ⁩—quoting the speaker’s own wife from years earlier in Skokie: a wife’s art is getting what she wants while making her husband think it was his idea\!)

**4\. Israel’s Sins, Exile, and the Wholly Faithful Lineage (⁧מִטָּה שְׁלֵמָה⁩)**

וְכֵן יִשְׂרָאֵל — הַחֲטָאִים הֵם עִקַּר בֵּרוּר שְׁלֵמוּתָם, **Similarly with Israel: their sins are central to the clarification of their perfection,** — וּלְכָךְ הֻצְרְכוּ לִגְלוֹת, **and therefore they had to go into exile,** — דֻּגְמַת יַעֲקֹב, הַבְּרִיחָה לְחוּץ לָאָרֶץ, **as Jacob needed to flee outside the Land,** — שֶׁהוּא בְּרִחוּק מֵהַכָּרַת יִצְחָק לְנֹכַח, **far from Isaac’s direct awareness of God.** — וְהוּא הַמְבָרֵר שְׁלֵמוּתוֹ,[^13] **This is what establishes his perfection.** — וְהַיְנוּ, כִּי שְׁלֵמוּתוֹ שֶׁמִּטָּתוֹ שְׁלֵמָה, **For his perfection is that his bed is whole, with all his offspring faithful,** — דְּבֵקוּת עַצְמוֹ בְּהַשֵּׁם יִתְבָּרַךְ, **expressing his own attachment to Hashem, blessed be He.** — וְאֵינוֹ שְׁלֵמוּת אֶלָּא כַּאֲשֶׁר גַּם בְּרִחוּק מָקוֹם לֹא זָז מִדִּבּוּקוֹ, **It is not perfection unless, even in a distant place, he never departs from that attachment.** — וּבְכָל מָקוֹם שֶׁגָּלוּ שְׁכִינָה עִמָּהֶם.[^14] **Wherever they were exiled, the Divine Presence accompanied them.** — וּלְכָךְ מִצַּד רִבְקָה, שֶׁהָיוּ הַבְּרָכוֹת לְיַעֲקֹב מִצִּדָּהּ גַּם כֵּן, **Therefore, through Rebecca, who also brought Jacob the blessings,** — הָיָה לוֹ הַגָּלוּת וְהַבְּרִיחָה, **he experienced exile and flight,** — כְּדֶרֶךְ שֶׁאָמְרוּ זִכְרוֹנָם לִבְרָכָה: **in keeping with our Sages’ statement:** — יִשְׂרָאֵל בָּעוֹלָם הַזֶּה אִי אֶפְשָׁר לָהֶם לְקַבֵּל רֹב טוֹבָה.[^15] **Israel in this world cannot receive an abundance of good.** — וְהַיְשׁוּעָה לְהַגִּיעַ עַל יְדֵי הַבְּרִיחָה לְזִוּוּגוֹ הוּא מִצַּד יִצְחָק, **The deliverance by which his flight led to marriage, however, came through Isaac,** — כְּמוֹ שֶׁכָּתַבְתִּי לְעֵיל כַּמָּה פְּעָמִים, **as I wrote above several times:** — שֶׁמִּיַּד יִצְחָק הוּא הַיְשׁוּעָה שֶׁאַחַר הַגָּלוּת.[^16] **the deliverance that follows exile comes through Isaac.** — וַהֲקָמַת הָאֻמָּה הָיָה רַק שָׁם בְּחוּץ לָאָרֶץ, **The nation was established specifically there, outside the Land,** — עַד הַתַּכְלִית שֶׁהוּא הַשְּׁנֵים עָשָׂר, **up to its culmination, the twelfth son.** — כִּי הַתַּכְלִית הוּא חוֹזֵר לַהַכָּרָה הַבְּרוּרָה שֶׁל אֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל, **For at the culmination one returns to the clear awareness associated with the Land of Israel.** — וְהוּא בִּנְיָמִין שֶׁזָּכָה לִמְקוֹם מִזְבֵּחַ,[^17] **This is Benjamin, who merited the site of the altar,** — שֶׁשָּׁם אֵשׁ שֶׁלְּמַעְלָה וְהַכָּרָה הַבְּרוּרָה בְּיִרְאָה, **where there is fire from above and clear awareness through awe.** — שֶׁזֶּהוּ הוּא רַק בְּאֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל, **This belongs specifically to the Land of Israel,** — שֶׁלָּכֵן יִצְחָק לֹא יָצָא חוּצָה לָאָרֶץ;[^18] **which is why Isaac never left the Land.** — וְכֵן לְעֵת קֵץ יָשׁוּבוּ לְאֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל. **Likewise, at the end they will return to the Land of Israel.**

**Shiur Commentary:** The word ⁧חֵטְא⁩ literally means “lacking” (⁧חִסָּרוֹן⁩). Rather than merely condemning a person, a ⁧חֵטְא⁩ acts as an ⁧עֵזֶר כְּנֶגְדּוֹ⁩ that exposes the exact ⁧חִסָּרוֹן⁩ corresponding to your personality that you need to repair—which is the inner mechanism of ⁧תְּשׁוּבָה⁩.

On ⁧יַעֲקֹב⁩’s ⁧מִטָּה שְׁלֵמָה⁩, the ⁧שִׁעוּר⁩ noted how ⁧יַעֲקֹב⁩’s blunt words to ⁧לָבָן⁩ (“⁧הָבָה אֶת אִשְׁתִּי⁩”) came from utter absence of ⁧תַּאֲוָה⁩ and pure ⁧לְשֵׁם שָׁמַיִם⁩ (a participant asked how such bluntness fits ⁧תִּפְאֶרֶת⁩ rather than ⁧יִצְחָק⁩’s ⁧גְּבוּרָה⁩, which the speaker acknowledged as a strong question while emphasizing the purity of intent). Furthermore, from ⁧וּבְכָל מָקוֹם שֶׁגָּלוּ שְׁכִינָה עִמָּהֶם⁩ (⁧מְגִלָּה כט ע״א⁩), the ⁧שִׁעוּר⁩ stressed that while we associate the ⁧שְׁכִינָה⁩ with the ⁧כּוֹתֶל⁩ and ⁧בֵּית הַמִּקְדָּשׁ⁩, its closest dwelling is inside our own ⁧לֵב⁩—a paradigm ⁧יַעֲקֹב⁩ established for our ongoing ⁧גָּלוּת⁩, where we rebuild our own “twelve ⁧שְׁבָטִים⁩” before returning to ⁧אֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל⁩ (⁧מַעֲשֵׂה אָבוֹת סִימָן לַבָּנִים⁩).

**5\. Building the Nation in Exile and Miraculous Shortening of the Way (⁧קְפִיצַת הַדֶּרֶךְ⁩)**

אֲבָל עִקַּר בִּנְיַן הָאֻמָּה בַּגָּלוּת בְּעֵת הָרִחוּק, **But the nation is principally built during exile, in the time of distance,** — שֶׁאָז הוּא תַּכְלִית הַיְגִיעָה וַעֲבוֹדָה וּשְׁלֵמוּת הָעוֹלָם הַזֶּה; **for that is when effort, divine service, and the perfection of this world reach their fullest expression.** — כִּי כַּאֲשֶׁר הוּא הַכָּרָה הַבְּרוּרָה, אָז אֵין שָׁם יְגִיעָה וַעֲבוֹדָה, **When there is clear awareness, there is no such toil and service.** — וְהוֹלָדַת הָאֻמָּה שְׁלֵמָה, שֶׁהוּא מִצַּד הָעֲבוֹדָה בִּבְחִירָה, **The birth of a complete nation, which comes through service freely chosen,** — הוּא רַק בְּעֵת הָרִחוּק יוֹתֵר.[^19] **occurs precisely in a time of greater distance.** — וּלְכָךְ הָיְתָה לוֹ קְפִיצַת הַדֶּרֶךְ,[^20] **Therefore the distance of his journey was miraculously shortened,** — עַל דֶּרֶךְ שֶׁאָמְרוּ זִכְרוֹנָם לִבְרָכָה: **in connection with what our Sages said:** — הַטִּפֵּשׁ אוֹמֵר, אֵימַת אֲנִי לוֹמֵד תּוֹרָה שֶׁבְּלִבּוֹ שֶׁל חָכָם וְכוּלֵי;[^21] **the fool says, “When will I learn the Torah in the sage’s heart?” and so forth.** — וּבֶאֱמֶת, ״אֲרֻכָּה מֵאֶרֶץ מִדָּהּ״,[^22] **Indeed, “its measure is longer than the earth,”** — אֲבָל בְּיַד הַשֵּׁם יִתְבָּרַךְ לְהַקְפִּיץ הַדֶּרֶךְ. **but Hashem, blessed be He, has the power to shorten the journey.** — וְכֵן בֶּאֱלִיעֶזֶר הָיְתָה קְפִיצַת הַדֶּרֶךְ,[^23] **Eliezer’s journey was likewise miraculously shortened,** — כִּי זִוּוּג הָאָבוֹת הַשְׁלָמַת צוּרָתָם, **for the Patriarchs’ marriages completed their spiritual form.** — וְזֶהוּ עַל דֶּרֶךְ אִלְמָלֵא הַקָּדוֹשׁ בָּרוּךְ הוּא עוֹזְרוֹ וְכוּלֵי,[^24] **This accords with the teaching, “Were the Holy One, blessed be He, not to help him,” and so forth:** — שֶׁהַהַשְׁלָמָה עֵזֶר הַשֵּׁם יִתְבָּרַךְ לְמַהֵר אֶת הַקֵּץ. **completion comes through the help of Hashem, blessed be He, who hastens the culmination.** — וְהוּא אוֹמֵר, ״כִּי קָרוֹב אֵלֶיךָ הַדָּבָר״ וְגוֹמֵר.[^25] **And Scripture says, “For the matter is near to you,” and so forth.**

**Shiur Commentary:** Real spiritual growth happens not in an easy, lavish life of effortless clarity, but in the distance of ⁧גָּלוּת⁩ where real ⁧בְּחִירָה⁩ and ⁧יְגִיעָה⁩ take place. At the same time, the distance can feel overwhelming—the ⁧תּוֹרָה⁩ truly is ⁧אֲרֻכָּה מֵאֶרֶץ מִדָּהּ⁩. That is where ⁧סִיעְתָּא דִּשְׁמַיָּא⁩ enters as ⁧קְפִיצַת הַדֶּרֶךְ⁩. Tying back to ⁧עֵזֶר כְּנֶגְדּוֹ⁩, there is a third partner beyond our spouse and our ⁧יֵצֶר הָרָע⁩: ⁧הַקָּדוֹשׁ בָּרוּךְ הוּא⁩ Himself is the ultimate ⁧עֵזֶר⁩ who bridges the distance and brings our effort to completion.

**6\. From Initial Fervor to Darkness: Rachel, Leah, and Returning Light**

וְקַבָּלַת הַבְּרָכוֹת הוּא דֻּגְמַת **Receiving the blessings corresponds to the saying,** — אֵין חֹזֶק כַּחֲסִידוּת בִּתְחִלָּתוֹ;[^26] **“There is no fervor like that of piety at its beginning.”** — וּמִיָּד אַחַר כָּךְ, ״וַיֵּצֵא״ וְכוּלֵי — עָרוֹם וְחָסֵר כֹּל,[^27] **Immediately afterward, “he departed,” and so forth, stripped of everything,** — עַד שֶׁמְּמַשֵּׁשׁ בַּחֹשֶׁךְ, **until he gropes in the darkness** — וּמַגִּיעַ לְתַכְלִיתוֹ, **and reaches his purpose:** — לְהִזְדַּוֵּג בַּשְּׁכִינָה בִּשְׁלֵמוּת הַלֵּב **to unite with the Divine Presence through the perfection of the heart** — (שֶׁהוּא הַנֶּעְלָם וְעָלְמָא דְּאִתְכַּסְיָא), **(which is inward and belongs to the concealed world),** — וּשְׁלֵמוּת הַמַּעֲשִׂים הַנִּגְלִים וְעָלְמָא דְּאִתְגַּלְיָא, **and through the perfection of outward deeds and the revealed world.** — שֶׁהֵם מַדְרֵגַת רָחֵל וְלֵאָה כַּיָּדוּעַ,[^28] **These are the levels of Rachel and Leah, as is known.** — וּמוֹלִיד שִׁבְטֵי יָהּ, **He then brings forth the tribes of God:** — רָצָה לוֹמַר, מַדְרֵגוֹת הַנַּפְשִׁיּוֹת הָרְאוּיוֹת כְּפִי שֹׁרֶשׁ נַפְשׁוֹ, **that is, the spiritual levels appropriate to the root of his soul.** — עַד שֶׁכְּשֶׁהוּא מַגִּיעַ קָרוֹב לְסוֹף מַדְרֵגָתוֹ, **When he draws near to the full attainment of his level,** — אָז חוֹזֵר וָאוֹר לוֹ כִּימֵי קֶדֶם בְּאֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל;[^29] **light returns to him, as in former days in the Land of Israel.** — וְגַם זֶה עַל יְדֵי רַמָּאוּת, **This too comes through cunning,** — בְּרִיחָה מִלָּבָן וּפַחַד מֵעֵשָׂו, **flight from Laban and fear of Esau,** — עַד שֶׁנִּשְׁקַט אַחַר כָּךְ. **until afterward he finds tranquility.**

**Shiur Commentary:** After the initial high of receiving the ⁧בְּרָכוֹת⁩, ⁧יַעֲקֹב⁩ goes out stripped of everything (in class, a participant clarified the ⁧מִדְרָשׁ⁩ of ⁧אֱלִיפַז⁩: ⁧יַעֲקֹב⁩ gave ⁧אֱלִיפַז⁩ all his money so that, as a poor man considered like dead, ⁧אֱלִיפַז⁩ would fulfill ⁧עֵשָׂו⁩’s command without killing him). That descent into darkness is the path to uniting two concurrent worlds that operate in our lives every day: ⁧עָלְמָא דְּאִתְגַּלְיָא⁩ (our visible daily deeds) and ⁧עָלְמָא דְּאִתְכַּסְיָא⁩ (the concealed inner reality behind mysteries like ⁧צַדִּיק וְרַע לוֹ וְרָשָׁע וְטוֹב לוֹ⁩). ⁧יַעֲקֹב⁩ had to marry both ⁧רָחֵל⁩ and ⁧לֵאָה⁩ to integrate both worlds simultaneously (note footnote 28 on the Zoharic pairing of ⁧לֵאָה⁩ with the concealed world and ⁧רָחֵל⁩ with the revealed world).

**Section 71 (אות ע״א) — וַיִּקַּח מֵאַבְנֵי הַמָּקוֹם**

**1\. The House of God (⁧בֵּית אֱלֹקִים⁩): Bounded Awareness vs. Mountain and Field**

״וַיִּקַּח מֵאַבְנֵי הַמָּקוֹם״.[^30] **“He took some of the stones of the place.”** — הַמָּקוֹם הוּא ״בֵּית אֱלֹקִים״; **The place is the “House of God”;** — אִם כֵּן הָאֲבָנִים — אַבְנֵי הַבַּיִת. **the stones, then, are the stones of that house.** — וְיַעֲקֹב הוּא שֶׁקְּרָאוֹ ״בַּיִת״,[^31] **Jacob was the one who called it a “house,”** — רָצָה לוֹמַר: מָקוֹם מֻגְדָּר וּמֻצְנָע, **meaning a bounded, secluded place,** — כִּי הַר וְשָׂדֶה יָכוֹל כָּל אֶחָד לִכָּנֵס בְּתוֹכָן. **for anyone can enter a mountain or a field.** — וְכֵן מֵאַבְרָהָם וְיִצְחָק יָצְאוּ עֲדַיִן פְּסֹלֶת, **Likewise, unfit offspring still emerged from Abraham and Isaac,** — כַּנִּזְכָּר לְעֵיל אוֹת ס״ה, שֶׁטּוֹעֲנִים ״לָנוּ הַמָּיִם״.[^32] **as explained above in section 65: they claim, “The water is ours.”** — מַה שֶּׁאֵין כֵּן יַעֲקֹב: **Jacob, however, represents** — בֵּרוּר צִמְצוּם הַהַכָּרָה דְּנֹכַח גָּמוּר בְּדֶרֶךְ הֶצְנֵעַ, **a clarified, focused awareness of being fully in His presence, attained in hiddenness,** — שֶׁאֵין נִגְלֶה כְּלָל לְאֻמּוֹת הָעוֹלָם; **which is not revealed at all to the nations of the world;** — אַךְ מִטָּתוֹ שְׁלֵמָה — רַק יִשְׂרָאֵל.[^33] **his offspring are wholly faithful—all of them Israel.** — וּ״פְרִיץ חַיּוֹת בַּל יַעֲלֶנָּה״.[^34] **“No ravenous beast shall ascend it.”** — וְזֶה שֶׁאָמְרוּ זִכְרוֹנָם לִבְרָכָה, **This is the meaning of our Sages’ statement** — שֶׁלָּקַח הָאֲבָנִים לְהִשָּׁמֵר מִן הַחַיּוֹת.[^35] **that he took the stones to protect himself from wild beasts.**

**Shiur Commentary:** The ⁧שִׁעוּר⁩ highlighted a striking ⁧חִדּוּשׁ⁩ here: one might assume that ⁧יִצְחָק⁩ (whose ⁧מִדָּה⁩ is ⁧גְּבוּרָה⁩) would be the most ⁧מֻגְדָּר⁩ (bounded), yet ⁧רַב צָדוֹק⁩ says that both ⁧אַבְרָהָם⁩ (⁧הַר⁩) and ⁧יִצְחָק⁩ (⁧שָׂדֶה⁩) were open terrain that anyone could enter, allowing ⁧פְּסֹלֶת⁩ to emerge. Only ⁧יַעֲקֹב⁩ built a ⁧בַּיִת⁩—fenced off and ⁧צָנוּעַ⁩—guarding something uniquely precious that belongs solely to ⁧כְּלַל יִשְׂרָאֵל⁩.

**2\. Stones at His Head (⁧וַיָּשֶׂם מְרַאֲשֹׁתָיו⁩), Sleep, Dreaming, and the Level of Shabbat**

״וַיָּשֶׂם מְרַאֲשֹׁתָיו״ — שֶׁהֵם שְׁמִירָה לְרֹאשׁוֹ, **“He placed them at his head”—they protect his head,** — כְּמוֹ שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר: ״כִּי לִוְיַת חֵן הֵם לְרֹאשֶׁךָ״;[^36] **as Scripture says, “For they are a graceful wreath for your head.”** — כִּי הָרֹאשׁ — שָׁם רֵאשִׁית הַמַּעֲשִׂים בַּמַּחֲשָׁבָה שֶׁבַּמֹּחַ. **For in the head lies the beginning of deeds, in the thought within the brain.** — וּלְכָךְ שָׂם אַבְנֵי וִיסוֹד הַבִּנְיָן, **He therefore placed the stones and foundation of the building there,** — לְהוֹלִיד בְּדוֹמֶה — מִטָּה שְׁלֵמָה.[^37] **to beget offspring in his likeness—a wholly faithful lineage.** — ״וַיִּשְׁכַּב שָׁם״ — שֶׁגַּם בְּשָׁכְבוֹ יֵדַע לִפְנֵי מִי וְכוּלֵי.[^38] **“He lay down there”—so that even while lying down he would know before Whom he lay, and so forth.** — ״וַיַּחֲלֹם״ —[^39] **“He dreamed” —** — שֶׁזֶּהוּ מַדְרֵגַת יַעֲקֹב, כַּנִּזְכָּר לְעֵיל אוֹת ס״א, **for this is Jacob’s spiritual level, as explained above in section 61:** — שֶׁמַּגִּיעַ לְמַדְרֵגָה שֶׁאֵין צָרִיךְ עוֹד עֲבוֹדָה וִיגִיעָה, **he reaches a level at which further labor and struggle are no longer needed,** — רַק הַשְּׁכִיבָה גַּם כֵּן טוֹב.[^40] **and even lying down is good.** — וְזֶהוּ בְּ״בַיִת״, שֶׁשָּׁם הַמְּנוּחָה, **This is the “house,” where there is rest,** — מַה שֶּׁאֵין כֵּן בְּהַר וְשָׂדֶה — יְגִיעָה.[^41] **whereas the mountain and field involve toil.** — וְלָכֵן אָמְרוּ זִכְרוֹנָם לִבְרָכָה: **Hence our Sages said,** — ״גּוֹי שֶׁשָּׁבַת חַיָּב מִיתָה״,[^42] **“A gentile who rests is liable to death,”** — שֶׁזֶּהוּ מַדְרֵגַת שַׁבָּת, **for this is the spiritual level of Shabbat,** — שֶׁיָּצָא מִשָּׁם יַעֲקֹב, וְזֶהוּ ״בְּאֵר שֶׁבַע״, כַּנִּזְכָּר לְעֵיל אוֹת מ״ח,[^43] **from which Jacob departed—namely, Beer-sheba, as explained above in section 48** — וְשָׁם אֵין מָקוֹם לְגוֹי, כַּנִּזְכָּר לְעֵיל. **and there a gentile has no place, as explained above.**

**Shiur Commentary:** Every action begins in the mind; placing the foundation stones around his head symbolizes guarding one’s thoughts so that the entire lineage flows from a pure mindset. ⁧רַב צָדוֹק⁩ also reveals the spiritual meaning of dreaming: unlike the constant toil of the mountain and field, the house represents ⁧מְנוּחָה⁩—the ⁧מַדְרֵגָה⁩ of ⁧שַׁבָּת⁩—where one’s inner orientation is so settled that even lying down and sleeping remain aligned with ⁧ה׳⁩.

**3\. Unifying the Two Stones: Havayah (⁧הֲוָיָה⁩), Time as One, and Shema Yisrael**

וְזֶה שֶׁאָמְרוּ זִכְרוֹנָם לִבְרָכָה: **This is the meaning of our Sages’ statement:** — ״אִם מִתְאַחוֹת שְׁתֵּי אֲבָנִים, יוֹדֵעַ אֲנִי שֶׁאֵין בּוֹ פְּסֹלֶת״.[^44] **“If the two stones join together, I know that there is no dross in him.”** — הַיְנוּ כִּי בִּנְיַן הַבַּיִת הוּא עַל יְדֵי הִתְחַבְּרוּת הָאֲבָנִים זוֹ עַל זוֹ, **That is, a house is built by joining the stones one upon another;** — שֶׁכָּל זְמַן שֶׁלֹּא נִתְחַבְּרוּ, אֵין בּוֹ מִשּׁוּם בּוֹנֶה וְסוֹתֵר, **as long as they have not been joined, the categories of building and demolishing do not apply,** — כְּמוֹ שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר בְּשַׁבָּת, פֶּרֶק הַבּוֹנֶה.[^45] **as discussed in Shabbat, in the chapter HaBoneh.** — וּכְשֶׁאֵין פְּסֹלֶת, אָז הוּא הַבַּיִת, **When there is no dross, there is the house:** — שֶׁהוּא הַיִּחוּד הַגָּמוּר בְּהֶצְנֵעַ. **complete union in hiddenness.** — שֶׁהַיִּחוּד הוּא שֶׁהַשֵּׁם יִתְבָּרַךְ מְיֻחָד בְּכָל הָעוֹלָם וְאֵין עוֹד מִלְּבַדּוֹ; **This unity means that Hashem is One throughout the world, with nothing besides Him;** — שֶׁזֶּהוּ הוֹרָאַת הֲוָיָה, הַמּוֹרָה לְיַעֲקֹב: **this is the meaning of the Name Havayah, which corresponds to Jacob:** — שֶׁהַהֹוֶה דֻּגְמַת הֶהָיָה וְיִהְיֶה — הַכֹּל אֶחָד, **the present is like the past and the future—all are one,** — אַחַר בְּרִיאַת הָעוֹלָם כְּעַד שֶׁלֹּא נִבְרָא הָעוֹלָם. **after the world’s creation just as before its creation.** — וְזֶהוּ יִחוּד ״שְׁמַע יִשְׂרָאֵל וְגוֹמֵר״ — ״יִשְׂרָאֵל סָבָא״, **This is the unity proclaimed in “Hear, O Israel,” and so forth—addressed to Israel the Patriarch,** — שֶׁזֶּהוּ מִדָּתוֹ: אֱמֶת וֶאֱמוּנָה; **whose attribute is truth and faithfulness;** — וְשׁוּב אֵין מָקוֹם לִפְסֹלֶת כְּלָל. **there is then no place at all for dross.** — וְזֶהוּ רֶמֶז יִחוּד שְׁתֵּי הָאֲבָנִים. **This is what the uniting of the two stones signifies.** — וּ״מִתְאַחִים״, רָצָה לוֹמַר: רֶמֶז לַבּוֹרֵא וְלַנִּבְרָא שֶׁמְּיֻחָד, **“Joining together” alludes to the Creator and the created being brought into unity;** — וְעַל יְדֵי זֶה הוּא בִּנְיַן הַבַּיִת, **through this the house is built,** — שֶׁהוּא הֶעְדֵּר קְרִיבַת הַפְּסֹלֶת לָזֶה, **and dross is prevented from approaching it,** — שֶׁהִיא תְּאוֹמָתוֹ כִּבְיָכוֹל, **for Israel is His twin, so to speak,** — כְּמוֹ שֶׁאָמְרוּ זִכְרוֹנָם לִבְרָכָה, בְּמִדְרַשׁ שִׁיר הַשִּׁירִים עַל פָּסוּק ״קוֹרוֹת בָּתֵּינוּ אֲרָזִים״: **as our Sages said in Midrash Shir HaShirim, on the verse “The beams of our houses are cedars”:** — ״מָה הַתְּאוֹמָה״ וְכוּלֵי;[^46] **“Just as a twin,” and so forth;** — וְכֵן הָאֲבָנִים הַמִּתְאַחִים.[^47] **so too the stones that join together.** — וְזֶה שֶׁאָמְרוּ זִכְרוֹנָם לִבְרָכָה: **This is also the meaning of our Sages’ words:** — ״אוֹתָן הָאֲבָנִים״ וְכוּלֵי, ״מָה רְטִיבָה הִרְטִיב״ וְכוּלֵי — **“Those stones,” and so forth; “What fresh growth did he bring forth?” and so forth** — צַדִּיקִים וְצַדִּיקוֹת, נְבִיאִים וּנְבִיאוֹת.[^48] **righteous men and women, prophets and prophetesses.** — לָשׁוֹן זֶה רָגִיל אֶצְלָם, כֵּן גַּם בְּפָרָשָׁה ע״ז, עַיֵּן שָׁם, וְכוּלֵי.[^49] **They commonly use this expression; it appears also in parashah 77—see there—and so forth.**

**Shiur Commentary:** The joining of the two stones mirrors both the ⁧הֲלָכָה⁩ of ⁧בּוֹנֶה וְסוֹתֵר⁩ and the deepest theological unity: the Creator and the created being uniting under ⁧אֵין עוֹד מִלְּבַדּוֹ⁩. In class, the line ⁧שֶׁהַהֹוֶה דֻּגְמַת הֶהָיָה וְיִהְיֶה הַכֹּל אֶחָד⁩ sparked a lively discussion about time—whether human beings truly experience the “present” or only past and future, whereas ⁧ה׳⁩ encompasses past, present, and future as one—and how ⁧יַעֲקֹב⁩ grasped that unity in ⁧שְׁמַע יִשְׂרָאֵל⁩.

**4\. Hidden Union (⁧יִחוּד בְּצִנְעָא⁩), the Lower Unity, and the Shechinah Called “I” (⁧אֲנִי⁩)**

שֶׁמִּצַּד הַיִּחוּד הַזֶּה, שֶׁאָמְרוּ זִכְרוֹנָם לִבְרָכָה עַל זֶה: **For through this union, concerning which our Sages said,** — ״בִּקֵּשׁ לְדַבֵּר עִם אוֹהֲבוֹ בְּצִנְעָא״,[^50] **“He wished to speak with His beloved in private,”** — שֶׁהַיִּחוּד הוּא בְּצִנְעָא, **since this union takes place in private,** — עַל יְדֵי זֶה הִרְטִיב שֶׁיָּצָא מִמֶּנּוּ צַדִּיקִים וְכוּלֵי. **he brought forth the fresh growth of righteous descendants, and so forth.** — כְּמוֹ שֶׁאָמְרוּ בְּפֶרֶק מָקוֹם שֶׁנָּהֲגוּ, **As they said in the chapter Makom SheNahagu:** — שֶׁהַבָּנִים אָמְרוּ ״שְׁמַע״ וְכוּלֵי, **the sons said, “Hear,” and so forth,** — וְהָאָב עָנָה ״בָּרוּךְ שֵׁם כְּבוֹד מַלְכוּתוֹ לְעוֹלָם וָעֶד״,[^51] **and their father answered, “Blessed be the name of His glorious kingdom forever and ever.”** — שֶׁהוּא יִחוּדָא תַּתָּאָה, **This is the lower unity,** — רָצָה לוֹמַר: יִחוּד הַשְּׁכִינָה בַּתַּחְתּוֹנִים,[^52] **meaning the union of the Divine Presence within the lower realms,** — ״בְּקֶרֶב אִישׁ וְלֵב עָמֹק״.[^53] **“within a person and a deep heart.”** — שֶׁזֶּהוּ ״וַיִּשְׁכַּב״ וְגוֹמֵר, **This is the meaning of “He lay down,” and so forth:** — שֶׁגַּם הַשְּׁכִיבָה כָּרָאוּי, **even his lying down was as it should be,** — עַד שֶׁ״וַיַּחֲלֹם״ וְכוּלֵי, **to the point that “He dreamed,” and so forth,** — שֶׁגַּם חֲלוֹמוֹתָיו וְכָל דִּמְיוֹנוֹתָיו וְהִרְהוּרֵי לִבּוֹ רַק ״וְהִנֵּה הַשֵּׁם״.[^54] **so that even his dreams, all his imaginings, and the thoughts of his heart were solely “And behold, Hashem.”** — זֶהוּ יִחוּדָא תַּתָּאָה שֶׁל הָאָב, **This is the father’s lower unity,** — שֶׁמִּזֶּה נִמְשָׁךְ שֶׁהַבָּנִים אוֹמְרִים ״שְׁמַע״ וְכוּלֵי, **from which it follows that the sons declare “Hear,” and so forth,** — שֶׁאֵין שׁוּם פְּסֹלֶת, כַּנִּזְכָּר לְעֵיל, רַק צַדִּיקִים וְכוּלֵי. **with no dross at all, as explained above, but only the righteous, and so forth.** — כִּי הַשְּׁכִינָה נִקְרֵאת ״אֲנִי״, **For the Divine Presence is called “I”:** — רָצָה לוֹמַר: שֶׁהָאָדָם מְדַבֵּר ״אֲנִי״ וְרוֹצֶה לוֹמַר הַשֵּׁם יִתְבָּרַךְ, **here, when a person says “I,” he means Hashem,** — שֶׁהַשֵּׁם יִתְבָּרַךְ שׁוֹכֵן בְּקִרְבּוֹ מַמָּשׁ — **for Hashem dwells within him:** — ״בְּקִרְבְּךָ קָדוֹשׁ״,[^55] **“The Holy One in your midst,”** — וּשְׁכִינָה מְדַבֶּרֶת מִגְּרוֹנוֹ. **and the Divine Presence speaks through his throat.** — וְאִם כֵּן, ״אִם אֲנִי לִי״ —[^56] **If so, “if I am for myself” —** — הֲרֵי הַכֹּל הַשֵּׁם יִתְבָּרַךְ הָעוֹשֶׂה,[^57] **it is Hashem Who does everything,** — וְהוּא הַמּוֹלִיד בְּדוֹמֶה לוֹ — רַק זֶרַע קֹדֶשׁ. **and He brings forth offspring in His likeness—only holy offspring.**

**Shiur Commentary:** When a parent’s inner life is completely saturated with awareness of ⁧ה׳⁩ (⁧יִחוּדָא תַּתָּאָה⁩—bringing the ⁧שְׁכִינָה⁩ down into the deepest heart), that inner truth transmits naturally to the children. The ⁧שִׁעוּר⁩ recalled the well-known post-Holocaust story of a ⁧רַב⁩ searching for hidden Jewish children who recited ⁧שְׁמַע יִשְׂרָאֵל⁩, immediately awakening their deepest memory of “⁧טַאטִי⁩” and “⁧מַאמִי⁩.” And when we learn ⁧תּוֹרָה⁩ right now, the ⁧שִׁעוּר⁩ noted, our truest “⁧אֲנִי⁩” is an extension of ⁧ה׳⁩—the ⁧שְׁכִינָה⁩ speaking from within our own throats.

**Section 72 (אות ע״ב) — וְהִנֵּה סֻלָּם מֻצָּב אַרְצָה**

**1\. The Ladder of Service: Ascent, Descent, and Sinai (⁧סוּלָם זֶה סִינַי⁩)**

וְהִנֵּה סוּלָּם מֻצָּב אַרְצָה —[^58] **“And behold, a ladder set upon the earth” —** — הוּא שְׁלִיבוֹת הַמַּדְרֵגוֹת בַּעֲבוֹדַת הַשֵּׁם יִתְבָּרַךְ, **these are the successive rungs in the service of Hashem, blessed be He,** — מִתְּהוֹם אַרְעָא עַד רוּם רְקִיעָא; **from the depths of the earth to the heights of heaven.** — וּמַלְאֲכֵי — שְׁלוּחֵי אֱלֹקִים — **“And the angels”—the emissaries of God —** — הֵם הַצַּדִּיקִים, **are the righteous,** — כְּמוֹ שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר: כִּי מַלְאַךְ הַשֵּׁם צְבָאוֹת הוּא.[^59] **as it is said: “For he is a messenger of Hashem of hosts.”** — עֹלִים וְיֹרְדִים — **“Ascending and descending” —** — כַּיָּדוּעַ שֶׁאִי אֶפְשָׁר לַעֲלִיָּה יוֹתֵר גְּדוֹלָה בְּלִי יְרִידָה; **as is known, a greater ascent is impossible without a descent.** — וּמִקּוֹדֶם עוֹלִים עַד אֵיזֶה מַדְרֵגָה, **First one ascends to a certain level,** — וְאָז הַיְרִידָה, **and then comes the descent.** — וְאַחַר כָּךְ: וְהִנֵּה הַשֵּׁם נִצָּב עָלָיו —[^60] **Afterward: “And behold, Hashem stands over him” —** — גַּם בַּיְרִידָה הַשֵּׁם נִצָּב, **even in the descent, Hashem is present,** — שֶׁגַּם זֶה לְהַכָּרַת הַשֵּׁם יִתְבָּרַךְ; **for this too serves the recognition of Hashem, blessed be He.** — וְהַשֵּׁם נִצָּב — מַדְרֵגָה יוֹתֵר גְּדוֹלָה עַל יְדֵי הַיְרִידָה כַּיָּדוּעַ.[^61] **“Hashem stands” thus expresses a still higher level reached through the descent, as is known.** — וְזֶה שֶׁאָמְרוּ זִכְרוֹנָם לִבְרָכָה: **This is what the Sages, of blessed memory, said:** — סוּלָּם — זֶה סִינַי, **“The ladder—this is Sinai,”** — שֶׁשָּׁם הוּא מְקוֹם עֲלִיַּת הַמַּדְרֵגוֹת **for there is the place of ascent through the levels,** — מִן הַפָּחוּת שֶׁבְּיִשְׂרָאֵל **from the lowest member of Israel** — עַד מַדְרֵגַת מֹשֶׁה רַבֵּינוּ עָלָיו הַשָּׁלוֹם שֶׁאֵין לְמַעְלָה מִמֶּנּוּ, **to the level of Moses our teacher, peace be upon him, above whom there is none,** — שֶׁהוּא עַל הָהָר.[^62] **who is upon the mountain.**

**Shiur Commentary:** The rungs of the ladder span every spiritual station from the lowest person in ⁧כְּלַל יִשְׂרָאֵל⁩ up to ⁧מֹשֶׁה רַבֵּינוּ⁩ on top of the mountain. Crucially, the angels ascend and then descend—because a higher ⁧עֲלִיָּה⁩ cannot happen without a ⁧יְרִידָה⁩. Even at the moment you fall, ⁧ה׳ נִצָּב עָלָיו⁩: ⁧ה׳⁩ is still standing right there, and recognizing His presence inside the ⁧יְרִידָה⁩ (including in ⁧גָּלוּת⁩) is what lifts a person to an even higher ⁧מַדְרֵגָה⁩.

**2\. Lowliness (“I Am with the Contrite”), Folding the Land, and the Patriarchs as Chariot**

וְתַכְלִית הַמַּדְרֵגוֹת: הַשֵּׁם נִצָּב עָלָיו — **The culmination of the levels is: “Hashem stands over him” —** — בִּהְיוֹתוֹ בְּשִׁפְלוּת וּבִירִידָה **when he is lowly and in a state of descent,** — גַּם כֵּן הַשֵּׁם לְנֶגְדּוֹ. **Hashem is still before him.** — וְזֶהוּ תַּכְלִית סִינַי, **This is the ultimate meaning of Sinai,** — כְּמוֹ שֶׁאָמְרוּ זִכְרוֹנָם לִבְרָכָה בְּסוֹטָה ה׳ עַמּוּד א׳, **as the Sages, of blessed memory, said in Sotah 5a,** — שֶׁנָּמוּךְ מֵהֶהָרִים; **that it is lower than the other mountains.** — וְאָמְרוּ שָׁם: **They also said there:** — אֲנִי אֶת דַּכָּא.[^63] **“I am with the contrite.”** — וְאָז מִתְקַפֵּל כָּל אֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל תַּחְתָּיו.[^64] **Then the entire Land of Israel is folded beneath him.** — אֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל הוּא מְקוֹם הַהַשָּׂגָה, **The Land of Israel is the realm of spiritual apprehension,** — וְכַאֲשֶׁר הוּא דַּכָּא כָּל כָּךְ וְשָׁפָל בְּעֵינָיו שֶׁאֵינוֹ כְּלוּם — **and when one is so contrite and lowly in his own eyes that he considers himself nothing,** — אָז הַשֵּׁם יִתְבָּרַךְ אוֹמֵר שֶׁהוּא הַכֹּל,[^65] **then Hashem, blessed be He, says that he is everything.** — שֶׁכָּךְ הַמִּשְׁפָּט, שֶׁלְּכָךְ אֲנִי אֶת דַּכָּא, **For this is the rule, and this is the meaning of “I am with the contrite”:** — שֶׁהַכֹּל שֶׁלּוֹ כִּבְיָכוֹל, **everything is his, as it were,** — כְּאִלּוּ גַּם הַשֵּׁם יִתְבָּרַךְ שֶׁלּוֹ. **as though even Hashem, blessed be He, were his.** — כַּיָּדוּעַ הַמָּשָׁל מֵהַשֵּׁם צִילְּךָ:[^66] **This is the familiar analogy drawn from “Hashem is your shadow”:** — כַּאֲשֶׁר הָאָדָם בְּשִׁפְלוּת, **when a person is lowly,** — כֵּן כִּבְיָכוֹל מַשְׁפִּיל עַצְמוֹ וְיָכוֹל לְהַשִּׂיגוֹ.[^67] **He too, as it were, lowers Himself, so that the person can apprehend Him.** — וְכָל מְקוֹם הַהַשָּׂגָה הוּא מְכוּפָּל תַּחְתָּיו, **The entire realm of apprehension is folded beneath him;** — הַכֹּל שֶׁלּוֹ עַל יְדֵי שֶׁהַשֵּׁם נִצָּב עָלָיו — עַל, בְּסָמוּךְ.[^68] **everything is his because “Hashem stands over him”—here “over” means beside him.** — אִי נָמֵי, עָלָיו **Alternatively, “over him”** — כְּעִנְיַן הָאָבוֹת הֵן הַמֶּרְכָּבָה, **has the sense of “the Patriarchs are the chariot,”** — שֶׁהֵם אֶבְרֵי הַהַנְהָגָה שֶׁל הַשֵּׁם יִתְבָּרַךְ, **for they are the organs through which Hashem, blessed be He, governs:** — רָצָה לוֹמַר, מַנְהִיגִים וּמַרְכִּיבִים לְהַשֵּׁם יִתְבָּרַךְ בָּעוֹלָם **that is, they conduct and bear His presence within the world** — כְּפִי תְּכוּנַת אֶבְרֵיהֶם וּמִדּוֹתֵיהֶם.[^69] **in accordance with the character of their faculties and attributes.**

**Shiur Commentary:** ⁧הַר סִינַי⁩ was chosen precisely because it was the lowest of mountains. By the principle of ⁧ה׳ צִלְּךָ⁩ (“⁧ה׳⁩ is your shadow”), when a person lowers himself in true humility, ⁧ה׳⁩ mirrors that lowliness and brings the entire realm of ⁧הַשָּׂגָה⁩ (clarity and spiritual grasp, symbolized by ⁧אֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל⁩) within reach—until the person becomes a ⁧מֶרְכָּבָה⁩, an instrument bearing ⁧ה׳⁩’s presence into the world.

**3\. Three Precious Gifts, “Anokhi” as Torah, Repentance (⁧תְּשׁוּבָה⁩), and Mashiach ben David**

וְנִבְטַח בְּשָׁלֹשׁ מַתָּנוֹת טוֹבוֹת: **He was assured of three precious gifts:** — אֶרֶץ — לְךָ אֶתְּנֶנָּה; **the Land—“I shall give it to you”;** — וְיִשְׂרָאֵל — וְהָיָה זַרְעֲךָ; **Israel—“Your offspring shall be”;** — וְהַתּוֹרָה — וְהִנֵּה אָנֹכִי עִמָּךְ.[^70] **and the Torah—“And behold, I am with you.”** — אָנֹכִי הוּא הַתּוֹרָה, **“I”—Anokhi—signifies the Torah,** — כְּדִבְרֵי רַבּוֹתֵינוּ זִכְרוֹנָם לִבְרָכָה בְּכַמָּה מְקוֹמוֹת, **as our Sages, of blessed memory, teach in several places,** — לְפִי שֶׁפּוֹתַחַת בּוֹ.[^71] **because it opens with that word.** — וְהַיְינוּ שֶׁדָּבָר זֶה הוּא רֵאשִׁית הַתּוֹרָה, **This means that this awareness is the beginning of Torah,** — שֶׁהַתּוֹרָה כֻּלָּהּ נִמְשֶׁכֶת אַחֲרָיו: **and the whole Torah follows from it:** — כַּאֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי עִמָּךְ תָּמִיד עִמּוֹ, **when the awareness “I am with you” is constantly with him,** — זֶה יָדוּעַ וְנוֹכַח עֵינָיו, **known to him and present before his eyes,** — מִמֵּילָא תּוֹרָה כֻּלָּהּ נוֹכַח עֵינָיו.[^72] **the whole Torah is consequently before his eyes.** — וַהֲשִׁבֹתִיךָ וְגוֹמֵר — הוּא תְּשׁוּבָה. **“I shall bring you back,” and so forth—this signifies repentance.** — כִּי לֹא אֶעֱזָבְךָ — **“For I shall not forsake you” —** — הוּא הַהַבְטָחָה כִּי לֹא יִדַּח, **this is the assurance that no one will be banished,** — שֶׁהוּא עַל יְדֵי הַתְּשׁוּבָה[^73] **which is realized through repentance,** — וּמַדְרֵגַת דָּוִד הַמֶּלֶךְ עָלָיו הַשָּׁלוֹם, **the spiritual level of King David, peace be upon him,** — שֶׁהֵקִים עוּלָּהּ שֶׁל תְּשׁוּבָה.[^74] **who established the yoke of repentance.** — וְהוּא מָשִׁיחַ הַמְקַבֵּץ וּמַשְׁלִים הַבְטָחַת כִּי לֹא אֶעֱזָבְךָ עַל יְדֵי תְּשׁוּבָה, **He is Mashiach, who gathers the dispersed and fulfills the promise “I shall not forsake you” through repentance,** — כְּמוֹ שֶׁאָמְרוּ בְּפֶרֶק חֵלֶק. **as the Sages said in the chapter Chelek.** — וּלְכָךְ בַּקָּשָׁתֵנוּ: הֲשִׁיבֵנוּ וְכוּלֵי, **Hence our plea: “Restore us,” and so forth,** — כְּהַבְטָחָתוֹ: וַהֲשִׁבֹתִיךָ.[^75] **in keeping with His promise: “I shall bring you back.”** — וּכְמוֹ שֶׁאָמְרוּ זִכְרוֹנָם לִבְרָכָה שָׁם, **As the Sages, of blessed memory, said there,** — מַעֲמִיד מֶלֶךְ וְכוּלֵי וּמַכְרִיחָם לִתְשׁוּבָה.[^76] **He establishes a king, and so forth, and compels them to repent.** — וְשָׁלֹשׁ הַבְטָחוֹת רִאשׁוֹנוֹת הֵם נֶגֶד אַבְרָהָם וְיִצְחָק וְיַעֲקֹב, **The first three promises correspond to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob,** — כְּמוֹ שֶׁכָּתַבְתִּי לְעֵיל כַּמָּה פְּעָמִים, **as I have written above several times,** — כִּי אַבְרָהָם יִצְחָק וְיַעֲקֹב וְדָוִד **for Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and David** — הֵם שְׁלֵימוּת כָּל הַמַּדְרֵגוֹת.[^77] **constitute the completion of all the spiritual levels.**

**Shiur Commentary:** Constant awareness of “⁧אָנֹכִי עִמָּךְ⁩” is the opening principle of the ⁧שֻׁלְחָן עָרוּךְ⁩—⁧שִׁוִּיתִי ה׳ לְנֶגְדִּי תָמִיד⁩—from which the entire ⁧תּוֹרָה⁩ flows naturally. Finally, the promise of return (⁧וַהֲשִׁבֹתִיךָ⁩ / ⁧כִּי לֹא אֶעֱזָבְךָ⁩) adds the fourth leg of the ⁧מֶרְכָּבָה⁩: ⁧דָּוִד הַמֶּלֶךְ⁩ and ⁧מָשִׁיחַ בֶּן דָּוִד⁩, who complete ⁧אַבְרָהָם⁩, ⁧יִצְחָק⁩, and ⁧יַעֲקֹב⁩ through ⁧תְּשׁוּבָה⁩, guaranteeing that no Jew is ever permanently lost. ⁧עַד כָּאן⁩.

[^1]:  Genesis 28:10. “Jacob departed from Beer-sheba and went to Haran.” Rav Tzadok reads the verse’s two movements, departing and going, as the two purposes that the passage will unite.

[^2]:  Genesis 27:43–46; 28:1–2. “Rebecca says, ‘Arise, flee to my brother Laban in Haran ... I will send and bring you from there.’ Isaac says, ‘Arise, go to Paddan-aram ... and take a wife there from the daughters of Laban, your mother’s brother.’” To Jacob, Rebecca explains the danger from Esau; to Isaac, she speaks of her distress over the Hittite women. Isaac’s stated reason for the journey is marriage. Rav Tzadok distinguishes these immediate intentions before tracing their common purpose.

[^3]:  Bereishis Rabbah 68:8; Matnot Kehunah ad loc. “The Sages say, ‘On that very day.’ Matnot Kehunah explains: ‘On the day he departed, on that same day he arrived in Haran.’” The shared day provides Rav Tzadok with the basis for joining departure and arrival into a single providential purpose.

[^4]:  Genesis 25:27. “Jacob was a man of integrity, dwelling in tents.” The description supplies the quality whose authenticity is tested when Jacob must deal with deception.

[^5]:  Numbers 23:23. “For there is no divination in Jacob and no sorcery in Israel.” Rav Tzadok applies the national designation “Jacob” to the patriarch’s quality, contrasting it with the divination associated here with the people of the East.

[^6]:  Kometz HaMinchah 2:50. “Love is specifically between two opposites ... perfection is the integration of both qualities.” In the section explicitly cited here, Sarah’s restraint complements Abraham’s love, while Rebecca’s kindness complements Isaac’s severity. Help from an opposing quality enables a person to include both qualities within a complete character.

[^7]:  Kometz HaMinchah 2:39. “Every trial is when one acts against one’s own desire and will; this shows the purity of one’s desire and expansive quality.” Abraham’s capacity for restraint proves that his love is holy rather than merely temperamental. The same logic explains why Jacob’s integrity must be established in circumstances that require its opposite. Ramban on Bereishis 22:1. “The One who tests, blessed be He, commands it in order to bring the matter from potential into action, so that he receives the reward of a good deed, not merely the reward of a good heart.” The trial develops a capacity through an actual choice. This complements Rav Tzadok’s sharper point: acting against one’s natural disposition establishes that even one’s characteristic virtue is freely devoted to Hashem.

[^8]:  Hosea 12:13. “Jacob fled to the field of Aram; Israel served for a wife, and for a wife he tended the flock.” The full verse joins flight with service for marriage, supporting the identification of the two aims of Jacob’s journey.

[^9]:  Bereishis Rabbah 71:2; Sefer HeArukh, s.v. mesubbin. “Bereishis Rabbah: ‘Most of the principal figures were Leah’s.’ The Arukh explains the word mesubbin here as ‘the great men.’” The phrase concerns Leah’s distinguished descendants. In the same passage, Jacob recognizes Leah as the mother of his children despite the deception at her marriage. The Midrash also calls Rachel the household’s mainstay; Rav Tzadok’s wording here emphasizes Leah’s foundational place through her children.

[^10]:  Bava Basra 123a; Maharsha, Chidushei Aggados, s.v. Ve-khi achi aviha. Jacob tells Rachel, “I am his brother in cunning.” When she asks whether the righteous may act thus, he answers, “With the pure You act purely, and with the crooked You act astutely.” Maharsha explains the two identities: “If for cunning, he is her father’s brother; if for righteousness, he is Rebecca’s son.” The response depends on what confronting Laban requires. This directly illuminates “where it is needed”: the discernment serves Jacob’s fidelity to Hashem.

[^11]:  Pri Tzaddik, Sukkot 12\. “Jacob too needed clarification in his quality, the quality of truth, because all his dealings with Esau appeared to involve deceit ... Outwardly, Jacob’s quality of truth was not clarified until he separated from Laban and Laban acknowledged him; then the quality of truth was clarified completely.” This close parallel treats the same episodes: the birthright, the blessings, and the rods. Their significance becomes clear through the completed course of Jacob’s dealings and his separation from Laban, when truth is established even within actions that appeared deceptive.

[^12]:  R. Tzadok, Divrei Soferim 28:2. “Jacob’s integrity was not due to an inborn inability to deceive; rather, it was because this was Hashem’s will.... This too was integrity, for the essence of integrity is to follow only Hashem’s will, as sheep follow the shepherd.” Rav Tzadok explicitly applies this to Laban and the blessings. The distinction is between fidelity to Hashem and merely being simple by temperament: the ability to act differently when required reveals what governs the person’s conduct.

[^13]:  Yoma 86b; Rashi, s.v. Aleihem hu yichyeh. “Great is repentance, for deliberate sins become merits for him.” The Gemara explains that this occurs through repentance from love. On “He shall live by them,” Rashi writes: “By everything he has done, even by the transgressions.” This supplies a precise framework for the perfection disclosed after failure: through loving return, the estrangement itself becomes part of a renewed bond. The transformation belongs to the repentance and the attachment it reveals.

[^14]:  Megillah 29a. “Come and see how beloved Israel is before the Holy One, blessed be He: wherever they were exiled, the Divine Presence was with them.” Rav Tzadok treats this enduring companionship as the national expression of Jacob’s attachment to God in a distant place. Exile reveals the depth of the bond. Haamek Davar on Bereishis 28:14, s.v. Ve-nivrechu vekha. “This would not have been recognizable had he always lived in tranquility ... But when he was subject to Laban, it became visible to the eye that Hashem’s providence was upon him.” Netziv explains that uninterrupted prosperity could have been attributed to good fortune. He describes the disclosure of divine care, while Rav Tzadok describes the disclosure of unwavering attachment; both locate that clarification in the difficulties of exile.

[^15]:  Yevamot 47a–47b. “They tell him: Know that the World to Come is prepared only for the righteous, and that Israel in the present time cannot receive either an abundance of good or an abundance of affliction.” The teaching occurs in the instruction of a prospective convert. Rav Tzadok takes its first limitation as a framework for the juxtaposition of Jacob’s blessings with subsequent exile.

[^16]:  Kometz HaMinchah 2:49. “All deliverances that come after distress arise from the quality of severity ... Therefore Isaac is the one who pleads for Israel’s redemption after exile ... After suffering, justice itself requires redemption.” The quality associated with Isaac brings both discipline and its healing conclusion. That is why Isaac’s instruction supplies the marriage that completes the journey begun as flight.

[^17]:  Genesis 35:16–18; Yoma 12a. “Genesis: ‘They journeyed from Bethel ... Rachel gave birth ... his father called him Benjamin.’ Yoma: ‘Therefore the righteous Benjamin merited becoming the host of the Divine Presence.’” Benjamin, the twelfth son, was born after the return to the Land. Yoma locates the sanctuary in Benjamin’s portion and describes his longing for the adjoining altar strip of Judah. The culminating son thus corresponds here to the return from exile and the dwelling of the Divine Presence.

[^18]:  Rashi on Genesis 26:2. “Do not descend to Egypt, for you are an unblemished burnt offering, and the territory outside the Land is not fitting for you.” Rashi supplies the traditional explanation for Isaac’s remaining in the Land. Rav Tzadok associates that sanctity with the clear awareness and awe represented by the altar.

[^19]:  Beit Yaakov, Vayetzei 72\. “Jacob needed to associate with him in order to bring the holiness out of him ... Then Hashem, blessed be He, told him, ‘Return to the land of your fathers’ ... There is no longer any task here for which you must remain.” R. Yaakov Leiner explains Jacob’s stay with Laban as the recovery of holiness through marriage and work. The return becomes possible when that task is complete. This complements Rav Tzadok’s account of the nation being formed through service in the place of distance. Ramchal, Derekh Hashem I:3:1. “If he were compelled in his actions to choose perfection inevitably, he would not truly be called the possessor of his perfection.” Ramchal therefore requires the ability to choose between opposing inclinations. This explains the value Rav Tzadok assigns to freely chosen service in the place of distance: fidelity maintained without overwhelming clarity becomes a perfection the person has willingly acquired.

[^20]:  Chullin 91b. “When he reached Haran, he said: Could I have passed the place where my fathers prayed without praying there? When he resolved to return, the earth contracted for him; immediately, ‘he encountered the place.’” This is the Talmud’s account of Jacob’s shortened journey. Rav Tzadok uses the image to describe divine help that brings a seemingly distant spiritual goal within reach.

[^21]:  Vayikra Rabbah 19:2. “R. Yohanan compares it to a loaf hanging in the air of a house. The fool says, ‘Who can bring it down?’ The intelligent person says, ‘Did someone else not hang it there?’ He brings two poles, joins them, and lowers it. Likewise, the fool says, ‘Who can learn the Torah in a sage’s heart?’ The intelligent person says, ‘Did he not learn it from someone else? I shall learn two teachings today and two tomorrow, until I learn the whole Torah.’” The Midrash answers discouragement with steady effort. Rav Tzadok adds that God can shorten the journey toward the very attainment for which a person must labor.

[^22]:  Job 11:9. “Its measure is longer than the earth and broader than the sea.” The verse describes the immeasurable scope of divine wisdom. Here it articulates the learner’s apparent distance from the goal before Rav Tzadok introduces divine assistance.

[^23]:  Bereishis Rabbah 59:11. “‘He arose and went to Aram-naharaim’: R. Isaac says, ‘On that very day’ ... ‘I came today to the spring’: I departed today and arrived today.” Eliezer’s mission brings Rebecca to Isaac. The same-day journey therefore supplies a second example of divine help toward the marriage that completes a patriarch’s spiritual form.

[^24]:  Sukkah 52b. “R. Shimon ben Lakish said: A person’s inclination gains strength against him each day and seeks to kill him, as it says, ‘The wicked watches the righteous and seeks to kill him.’ Were the Holy One, blessed be He, not to help him, he could not overcome it, as it says, ‘Hashem will not leave him in its hand, nor condemn him when he is judged.’” The source makes divine aid indispensable within human struggle. Rav Tzadok applies this to the final completion that a person’s exertion alone cannot secure.

[^25]:  Deuteronomy 30:11–14. “This commandment that I command you today is not beyond your reach and is not far away. It is not in heaven, that you should say, ‘Who will ascend to heaven for us, fetch it for us, and let us hear it, so that we may do it?’ Nor is it beyond the sea, that you should say, ‘Who will cross the sea for us, fetch it for us, and let us hear it, so that we may do it?’ For the matter is very near to you, in your mouth and in your heart, to do it.” The surrounding verses make the contrast explicit: what seems remote is accessible in speech, heart, and action.

[^26]:  Rokeach, Introduction, as quoted in Tzidkat HaTzadik 149\. “No strength surpasses that of piety at its beginning.” Rav Tzadok quotes this maxim from the Rokeach in Tzidkat HaTzadik 149\. Here he applies it to the powerful initial illumination represented by Jacob’s blessings. The following loss and struggle make that initial gift into an acquired spiritual achievement.

[^27]:  Bereishis Rabbah 68:2. “When Eliezer went to bring Rebecca, what is written? ‘The servant took ten camels.’ But I have not even one nose-ring or one bracelet. R. Hanina says: He sent him destitute. R. Yehoshua ben Levi says: He sent possessions with him, but Esau took them from him.” The Midrash voices Jacob’s distress at the contrast with Eliezer’s well-equipped mission. It continues with his refusal to despair of his Creator: “My help is from Hashem.” This explains the loss that follows the blessings.

[^28]:  Zohar II, 29b (Vaera). “Rachel and Leah are two worlds: one a concealed world and one a revealed world ... One was buried and hidden within the cave ... the other stands openly at the crossroads.” Leah corresponds to the concealed world and inward perfection; Rachel corresponds to the revealed world and outward deeds. The familiar order of their names in Rav Tzadok’s sentence does not reverse that correspondence.

[^29]:  Bereishis Rabbah 68:10. “The Holy One, blessed be He, caused the sun to set before its time in order to speak privately with our father Jacob ... When did He restore the two hours by which He had made the sun set as Jacob left his father’s house? On his return to his father’s house, as it is written, ‘The sun shone for him.’” The Midrash compares the early sunset to a king extinguishing the lamps so he can speak privately with his friend. Darkness thus enables intimacy, and the light withheld at departure returns afterward. This supplies the narrative pattern of concealment, union, and renewed illumination. Tzidkat HaTzadik 149\. “But this is only a vision; effort is needed until it becomes a possession, and everything one apprehends is absorbed in the heart ... When one reaches the fullness of acquisition, one merits a second vision, for one returns to the beginning.” Rav Tzadok there quotes the same saying from the Rokeach and distinguishes initial inspiration from a quality made one’s own through effort. The second vision explains why the light returning at the end of this passage is the fulfillment of the beginning.

[^30]:  Genesis 28:11. “He took some of the stones of the place, placed them at his head, and lay down in that place.” The stones, the place, and Jacob’s sleep are the successive details interpreted throughout this section. Verse 17 calls the place the House of God.

[^31]:  Pesachim 88a:3-4. “Not like Abraham, of whom ‘mountain’ is written ... and not like Isaac, of whom ‘field’ is written ... but like Jacob, who called it a ‘house.’” The Gemara distinguishes the three patriarchal descriptions of the sanctuary.

[^32]:  Kometz HaMinchah 2:65. “From Abraham and Isaac there was dross—Esau and Ishmael ... therefore they disputed over these two wells, saying, ‘The water is ours’ ... but the third well, Rechovot, corresponds to Jacob.” Rav Tzadok’s explicit earlier discussion treats the wells as spiritual qualities and the claim to their water as a claim to the patriarchs’ inheritance. His contrast here concerns the completed spiritual household of Jacob.

[^33]:  Mei HaShiloach, Volume II, Talmud, Pesachim 88a:1. “This is our father Jacob, who was enclosed on every side ... ‘Since I labor to recognize Hashem, I will not bequeath this to outsiders.’ This is called a house, through which no outsider passes.” R. Mordechai Yosef Leiner interprets the same house image as the guarded transmission of an inward knowledge of God. This closely parallels Rav Tzadok’s movement from hidden awareness to Jacob’s wholly faithful household.

[^34]:  Isaiah 35:9. “No lion will be there, nor will a ravenous beast ascend it ... the redeemed will walk there.” The verse describes the protected road of redemption. Rav Tzadok applies its image to the exclusion of spiritual dross from Jacob’s house.

[^35]:  Bereshit Rabbah 68:11. “He arranged them like a trough and placed it beneath his head, because he feared the wild beasts.” The Midrash explains the stones as a protective enclosure. Rav Tzadok reads that protection inwardly, as guarding the origin of thought and action.

[^36]:  Proverbs 1:8-9. “Hear, my son, your father’s instruction, and do not forsake your mother’s teaching; for they are a graceful wreath for your head.” The preceding verse identifies the teaching received from one’s parents. The image of what surrounds the head thus also fits Rav Tzadok’s theme of a holy lineage founded in the mind.

[^37]:  Ohr Zarua LaTzadik, The Egyptian Exile 4:7. “A house means that the entire household has been chosen; even day-old infants belong to this house ... Jacob therefore called it a house, for from Jacob came a wholly faithful lineage without dross ... fixed and enduring.” This is a particularly close explanation of “a wholly faithful lineage”: the house signifies a lasting identity transmitted to all its children, beyond the achievement of one exceptional individual.

[^38]:  Rambam, Mishneh Torah, Hilchos De’os 3:3. “Even while he sleeps, if he sleeps deliberately so that his mind and body may rest ... his sleep is found to be service of the Omnipresent, blessed be He.” Rambam makes this depend on restoring one’s capacity to serve Hashem. This gives a practical counterpart to Jacob’s holy rest: even an involuntary bodily state belongs within avodah when its governing purpose is awareness and service of Hashem.

[^39]:  Genesis 28:12. “He dreamed: a ladder was set upon the earth, with its head reaching heaven.” The revelation comes during Jacob’s sleep: even rest can express his completed awareness of God.

[^40]:  Kometz HaMinchah 2:61. “Rather, like Jacob, who called it a house, bounded and protected on every side ... when the evil inclination will be slain ... for Jacob represents the slaying of the inclination and is wholly good.” This is the explicit antecedent of the present claim about rest. Rav Tzadok describes the culmination of refinement, when inner opposition has been overcome; he is not describing the starting point of spiritual work. Ramchal, Mesillat Yesharim 26\. “Every use they make of worldly things after they are already attached to His holiness is an elevation ... for the thing that has merited serving the righteous person.” Ramchal illustrates this with Jacob’s stones, each wishing to support his head. Settled holiness enables bodily activity to elevate what serves the person. The chapter begins, “Its beginning is effort and its end a gift,” and requires the earlier stages of disciplined service. Jacob’s rest thus expresses completed avodah.
